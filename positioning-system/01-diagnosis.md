## §B. STOP — four résumé claims your own files do not support

I am raising this first because it is the only thing in this system that can
actively destroy the other five months of work, and because it is cheap to fix.

Cincinnati private capital is a small, high-trust, heavily overlapping market:
ACG Cincinnati, CFA Society Cincinnati, the Goering Center and UC Lindner share
members constantly [R]. A UC alum reading your résumé is one of the most likely
readers you will have. Anything the university's own record contradicts will be
caught by exactly the people you most want to impress — and it will be read not
as puffery but as a character signal, because the job you want is one where
people hand you money on the strength of your word.

### B1 — "M.S., Financial Mathematics" ❌

Your transcript and diploma say **Business Administration, Finance Program**,
Carl H. Lindner College of Business. Your degree file is literally `MSFin Degree.pdf`.
"Financial Mathematics" is a different degree, and UC's registrar can be checked
in one call. You *do* hold a Graduate Certificate in Quantitative Finance [V] —
which is where the quantitative signal legitimately lives.

**[REC] Correct to:**
> M.S., Finance — Carl H. Lindner College of Business, University of Cincinnati
> Graduate Certificate, Quantitative Finance — University of Cincinnati

Risk if unfixed: **severe**. Degree title is the single most verified item on any
résumé and is checked in essentially every background screen.

### B2 — "valuation within 10% of analyst consensus" ❌

Your capstone concluded **SELL with 23.4% implied downside** — intrinsic $391.36
against a $510.58 market price, a 30.5% gap [V]. Neither the 29-page report nor
the deck contains an analyst-consensus comparison, and a valuation 23% below
market is not "within 10% of consensus." The claim also undercuts the work: the
interesting thing about your capstone is that you took a *contrarian* position and
defended it with a sensitivity band ($295–$565) showing the call flips on a 50bp
WACC move. That is a better story than accuracy-to-consensus, which is not a
virtue in this industry anyway.

**[REC] Correct to:**
> Capstone: multi-scenario DCF valuation of S&P Global (team of four). Concluded
> SELL at a $391 intrinsic value against a $510 market price; scenario band of
> $295–$565 demonstrated the recommendation's sensitivity to a 50bp move in WACC
> and 50bp in terminal growth.

Also add "(team of four)". It was [V] a group capstone; presenting it as solo work
is a trivially discoverable overstatement and costs you nothing to disclose.

### B3 — "CFA Level I — 2025 Scholarship Awardee" listed under *Certifications* ⚠️

An access scholarship is an **exam-fee award, not a credential and not a passed
exam** [R]. Listed among BMC and other certifications, it reads as an attainment.
In a city with an active CFA society whose members will be across the table from
you, this is the worst possible audience for that ambiguity.

**[REC]** Move it out of Certifications and state your actual status precisely —
one of:
> CFA Program: Level I candidate, February 2027 sitting (2025 Access Scholarship recipient)

or, if you have sat and passed, say so with the date; if you have not registered,
say "Access Scholarship recipient, 2025" under Awards and nothing more.

### B4 — "Authorized to work in the U.S. with no sponsorship required" 🚨

Your résumé asserts present work authorization. You have told me you are **not
currently authorized** and are awaiting an EAD. A résumé is a document an employer

I am not your lawyer and this is not legal advice. But the gap between the

## §F. Your natural market — where you are unusually useful

The question is not "where would I like to work" but **"where is a person with my
exact evidence worth more than the alternative candidate?"**

You are worth *less* than the alternative at a bulge bracket, where the
alternative is a target-school junior with a summer analyst offer.

You are worth **more** than the alternative at a **two-to-six-person Cincinnati
firm that buys or lends to companies doing $2M–$20M of EBITDA**, because there
the alternative candidate is a bank analyst who has never seen a company without a
data room, cannot build from bank statements, has never written an LP document,
and needs visa sponsorship the firm cannot administer. You beat that person on
four dimensions simultaneously.

**Concentric target definition, in priority order:**

1. Independent sponsors and deal-by-deal platforms in Greater Cincinnati.
2. LMM PE funds under ~$300M AUM within a two-hour drive.
3. Searchers and LDE operators anywhere in the Midwest (remote-friendly, and the
   population is growing fast [R]).
4. SBIC / mezzanine / private credit shops in Ohio, Kentucky, Indiana.
5. Middle-market M&A boutiques in Cincinnati, Columbus, Indianapolis, Louisville.
6. Valuation & transaction advisory practices, Cincinnati offices — the floor.

Everything else is opportunistic. `14-ead-arrival-and-100-day.md` names the
thirty specific employers.
