## §1. Weak ties and job mobility

**Claim.** Moderately weak ties transmit job opportunities better than strong
ties — but the relationship is an inverted U, not a straight line.

**Evidence.** Rajkumar, Saint-Jacques, Bojinov, Brynjolfsson & Aral, *A causal
test of the strength of weak ties*, **Science** (2022). Multiple large randomised
experiments on LinkedIn's "People You May Know" over five years, varying weak-tie
prevalence across **20M+ people, 2B new ties, 600k job transitions**. Found
weaker ties increased job transmission **up to a point**, with diminishing
returns beyond it; the most useful ties were moderately weak — roughly ten shared
connections, infrequent interaction.
<https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abl4476>

**Confidence: high.** The largest causal test of Granovetter's 1973 hypothesis
that exists. Limits: one platform, and job transmission generally rather than
private-markets hiring specifically.

**Application.** Your B tier — the connectors — is where the return is, not the
A tier and not a mass of strangers. This is why Sheet 2 tiers relationships and
enforces a 60-day cadence on B. **[I]** Note the correction to the popular
version: "collect weak ties" is *not* what the study found. Very weak ties are
worse. The target is the maintained acquaintance.

## §2. Networking and career outcomes over time

**Claim.** Networking behaviour predicts both current salary and the *rate* of
salary growth; building and maintaining contacts is distinguishable from using
them.

**Evidence.** Wolff & Moser, *Effects of networking on career success: A
longitudinal study*, **Journal of Applied Psychology** 94(1), 196–206 (2009).
Longitudinal, multi-wave, six networking subscales crossing internal/external
with building/maintaining/using.
<https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19186904/>

**Confidence: moderate-high.** Longitudinal design is a real improvement on the
cross-sectional literature. Limits: German sample, self-reported networking,
salary as the outcome.

**Application.** The *maintenance* behaviour is what compounds — which is
precisely the behaviour that dies without a system. This is the justification for
the CRM cadence fields and the daily follow-up priority.

## §3. Referrals

**Claim.** Referred candidates are meaningfully better outcomes for employers,
which is why a referral changes your odds.

**Evidence.** Burks, Cowgill, Hoffman & Housman, *The Value of Hiring through
Employee Referrals*, **Quarterly Journal of Economics** 130(2), 805–839 (2015).
Nine large firms across call centres, trucking and software. Referred workers
**10–30% less likely to quit**, better on rare high-impact metrics, higher profit
per worker; effect concentrated in referrals from high-productivity employees.
<https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/130/2/805/2330001>

**Confidence: high** for the finding; **inference** for the transfer to
lower-middle-market private capital, which the study did not examine.

**Application.** The 100-day target is 20 referred applications, not 40 cold
ones. It also explains why an A-tier relationship must be built until the person
would genuinely *want* to spend credibility on you — a referral is not a favour,
it is a bet.

## §4. Deliberate practice — the honest version

**Claim.** Practice matters, but far less than the popular account claims, and
almost not at all in professional domains.

**Evidence.** Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald, *Deliberate practice and performance
in music, games, sports, education, and professions: A meta-analysis*,
**Psychological Science** 25(8), 1608–1618 (2014). Deliberate practice explained
**26%** of variance in games, 21% music, 18% sports, **4% education, and under 1%
in professions**. When only log-based (rather than retrospective) practice
measures were used, it explained about **5%** overall.
<https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0956797614535810>

**Confidence: high**, and it is contested — Ericsson disputed the operationalisation.

**Application, and this one is uncomfortable.** [I] Grinding modelling reps is
**not** the highest-return use of your hours. In professional domains the
variance is dominated by other things — and in private markets those things are
access, judgment, reputation and deal flow. This is a direct argument for the
allocation in this system: roughly 45 minutes a day on relationships against
75–90 minutes on producing evidence, rather than three hours a day on technical
drilling. It is also why `01-diagnosis.md` §H concludes your problem is
legibility and access rather than capability.

## §5. Spaced repetition

**Claim.** Distributing study across time beats massing it, and the optimal gap
grows with how long you need to retain.

**Evidence.** Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted & Rohrer, *Distributed practice in
verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis*, **Psychological
Bulletin** 132(3), 354–380 (2006). 839 assessments across 317 experiments in 184
articles. The inter-study interval producing maximal retention **increased as the
retention interval increased**.
<https://www.yorku.ca/ncepeda/publications/CPVWR2006.html>

**Confidence: high** for verbal recall. **[I]** Transfer to complex professional
skills like valuation judgment is an inference, not a finding — spacing clearly
helps you retain the WACC formula; whether it improves your judgment about
normalised EBITDA is not established.

**Application.** Sheet 9's expanding `Interval Days` (3 → 7 → 16 → 35 → 70).
Use it for factual and formulaic material; do not expect it to substitute for
doing the work.

## §6. Implementation intentions

**Claim.** Specifying *when, where and how* substantially increases follow-through
versus intention alone.

**Evidence.** Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006), meta-analysis of **94 independent
tests, 8,000+ participants**: **d = 0.65**, medium-to-large, and robust to
publication-bias adjustment. Extended by Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer (2024)
across 642 tests, framing the effect as contingent on plan format and motivation
rather than a single universal value.
<https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/bookseries/abs/pii/S0065260106380021>

**Confidence: high**, with the 2024 caveat that the effect varies by context.

**Application.** This is the best-evidenced item in the entire system, and it is
free. It is why the daily playbook specifies times and durations, why events get
a 48-hour pre-brief block on the calendar, and why `16` §3 tells you to calendar
the pre-statement payment. **[I] If you implement one thing from this file, put
the blocks on the calendar.** It is better evidenced than any of the software.

## §7. Habit formation

**Claim.** Habits take longer than folklore says, vary enormously, and — most
usefully — **missing a day does not undo them**.

**Evidence.** Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle, *How are habits formed:
Modelling habit formation in the real world*, **European Journal of Social
Psychology** 40(6), 998–1009 (2010). 96 participants, 12 weeks. Median **66 days**
to 95% of asymptotic automaticity **among the 48% who reached it**, range
**18–254 days**. A single missed day did not derail formation.
<https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.674>

**Confidence: moderate.** Small sample, simple behaviours (eating, drinking,
walking), and less than half of participants reached automaticity at all. The
"66 days" figure is routinely over-quoted as a rule; it is a median among
successes.

**Application.** Design for **recovery, not streaks** — hence the 10-minute
minimum viable day in `12-daily-weekly-os.md` §7. **[I]** And note the honest
implication: a 2.5-hour daily routine involving judgment and social risk is far
more complex than the behaviours studied here, so do not expect it to become
automatic. Expect to rely on the calendar and the dashboard indefinitely. That is
not a failure of discipline; it is what the evidence predicts.

## §8. Search fund returns — read the fine print

**Claim.** The headline search-fund return is real as an aggregate and misleading
as an expectation.

**Evidence.** Stanford GSB **2026 Search Fund Study** — 862 core funds since 1984,
~99% of known US/Canada funds, data through 2025-12-31: **33.9% aggregate IRR,
4.75x ROI**; ~58% acquire historically but only **48% of the 2021–2024 cohorts**;
67 long-duration enterprises tracked, **63% launched in 2024 or later**.
<https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/2026-search-fund-study-selected-observations>

Secondary analyses (lower-tier sources, **verify before quoting**) put the
aggregate closer to **2.8x**, report a Yale working paper finding no individual
investor in its sample replicated the index, and **58% of deal-level returns
under 2x**.

**Confidence: high** for the Stanford figures (Tier 1, the institution's own
publication). **Low-to-moderate** for the secondary critiques — treat as leads to
verify, not as facts.

**Application.** Two uses. It sets your own expectations honestly
(`15-business-preparation.md` §2). And it is your best conversational
differentiator (`08-conversation-portfolio.md` §5) — in a room where everyone
quotes 4.75x, being the person who read the cohort data is memorable.
