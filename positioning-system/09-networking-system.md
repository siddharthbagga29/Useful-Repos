# PART VIII (cont.) — Networking System

## §1. Event SOP

### 48 hours before
- [ ] Get the attendee or registrant list if published; if not, get the speaker,
      sponsor and host-committee lists — always published, and higher-value anyway
- [ ] Identify **5–10 priority people**; add each to Sheet 2 via the Sourcing Protocol
- [ ] For each: one specific thing about their firm, sourced, that you could ask about
- [ ] Add the event's pre-brief block to your calendar

### 24 hours before
- [ ] Narrow to **3 must-meets** and 4 nice-to-meets. Three is the realistic
      number for a two-hour event if you want real conversations
- [ ] Write the one question you will ask each of the three (`08` §6)
- [ ] Re-read your own Project 1 one-pager. **[I]** You need one live, specific
      thing you are working on; "I'm looking for opportunities" is not it
- [ ] Confirm dress code by asking the organiser. Cincinnati LMM skews
      business-casual; overdressing at a brewery happy hour is its own signal

### Day of — the pack
1. 10-second and 30-second intro, rehearsed out loud that morning (`02` §3)
2. Three questions, memorised
3. Two investment topics you can carry for five minutes (`08` §4 themes 1 and 4)
4. Your Project 1 status, in one sentence
5. One genuine achievement, stated plainly, without inflation [V, A10]
6. Phone with Sheet 2 open; a pen and paper card is better if the room allows it

### At the event — mechanics
- **Arrive early.** The first twenty minutes are the only time senior people are
  unattached. **[I]** This is the highest-return fifteen minutes of the entire
  evening and almost everyone wastes it.
- Ask, then listen. Aim for a 30/70 talk ratio.
- **Never ask for a job at an event.** Ask for a *follow-up conversation about a
  specific thing* — the ask is "could I send you the sector data I mentioned?"
- Write three words on the back of each card immediately after the conversation.
  You will not remember. Everyone believes they will.
- Leave while it is still good. Four real conversations beats twelve exchanges.

### Within 24 hours (non-negotiable)
- [ ] Log every contact in Sheet 2 with topic, their interests, next action
- [ ] Send each a personalised follow-up — **reference the specific thing they
      said**, not "great to meet you"
- [ ] If you promised something, send it *in that same message*
- [ ] Set Next Contact date and cadence

### Within 7 days — follow up with value
Send something they would have wanted even if they had never met you: a data cut
from your sector work, a filing relevant to their portfolio, an introduction to
someone useful.
**[I]** This is the step that converts an acquaintance into a B-tier relationship,
and it is the step essentially nobody does. It is also why the portfolio projects
exist — they are the inventory you draw value from.

### Within 30 days — reconnect
Short, no ask. "Finished the piece I mentioned — attaching it. No response
needed." **[I]** A message that explicitly requires nothing is the most reliable
way to stay present without becoming a cost.

## §2. Outreach scripts

### Cold — the Curiosity message (default)

> **Subject:** Question about [specific sector] deal flow in Cincinnati
>
> [Name] — I'm a UC Lindner MS Finance grad in Cincinnati, working through a
> lower-middle-market thesis on [sector] in the four-county region. I've been
> pulling County Business Patterns data on establishment counts by employee band,
> and I'm getting [specific finding].
>
> You've invested in [specific company or sector] — does that match what you see
> in your own sourcing, or am I reading the data wrong?
>
> Happy to send the analysis either way. I'm mid green-card process so I'm not
> job-hunting at the moment; I'm just trying to understand this market properly
> before I am.
>
> Siddharth

Why it works: a real question they are uniquely qualified to answer; evidence you
did work before writing; an explicit non-ask that removes the defensive read;
status disclosed without apology.

### Warm — UC alumni

> **Subject:** Fellow Lindner grad — question on LMM underwriting
>
> [Name] — I saw you did your [degree] at Lindner. I finished the MS Finance
> there in 2025 and I'm now working through a lower-middle-market thesis on
> [sector] here in Cincinnati.
>
> One question I keep getting stuck on: [specific, genuinely difficult question].
>
> Would you have fifteen minutes in the next couple of weeks? Happy to work
> around whatever's convenient.

### Post-event

> [Name] — good to meet you at [event]. You mentioned [specific thing they said];
> I went and looked at [related thing] afterwards and found [specific finding] —
> attaching it in case it's useful.
>
> No reply needed. I'll keep an eye out for the next [org] event.

### The ask for a critique (highest response rate)

> [Name] — I've finished a [sector] thesis for the Cincinnati LMM market, built
> off Census and BLS county data. You'd know immediately where I've got it wrong.
>
> Would you be willing to read eight pages and tell me the two things that are
> most incorrect? I'd rather hear it now than repeat it in a room.

**[I]** This works better than any other message in this file, for a reason worth
understanding: it asks for expertise rather than favour, it is bounded and
specific, it flatters accurately, and it gives them a role — critic — that senior
people enjoy. It also converts naturally into a second conversation, because you
owe them a revised version.

### What never to send
- "Can I pick your brain?" — signals extraction with no return
- "I'd love to learn about your firm" — their website exists
- A résumé attached to a first cold email — reframes you from peer to applicant
- Anything over 150 words
- Anything sent to more than one person at the same firm in the same week

## §3. Reputation strategy — ranked by return

Ranked on effort × credibility × reach × relationship-creation × hiring impact.

| Rank | Activity | Effort | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Original sector research, shared 1:1 with named people** | High | Highest credibility per hour. Creates a *relationship*, not an audience. **[I] This is the one to do.** |
| 2 | **Showing up repeatedly at the same 3 organisations** | Medium | Familiarity compounds; the fourth time you meet someone, you are a known quantity |
| 3 | **Asking practitioners to critique your work** | Low | See §2. Reputation and relationship in one action |
| 4 | **Publishing that same research publicly (LinkedIn article, simple site)** | Low marginal | Makes you findable after the room — closes the §D5 gap |
| 5 | **Speaking / presenting to a student or small professional group** | Medium | UC Lindner clubs and the Johnson Investment Institute are realistic venues for a recent alum |
| 6 | **CFA Level I completion** | Very high | Real but slow signal. Worth it *if* you commit; see §4 |
| 7 | **Volunteering at ACG / CFA Society events** | Low | Gets you into the room and next to organisers. Underrated |
| 8 | **Frequent short LinkedIn posts** | Low | Reach without depth. Do it as distribution for #1, never as the product |
| 9 | **Commenting on others' posts** | Very low | Marginal, but cheap and it keeps you visible |
| 10 | **Certifications other than CFA** | High | Low return for your specific paths. Skip for now |

**[I] The strategic point:** items 1–3 build *relationships*, items 4 and 8 build
*audience*. For private markets, relationships are worth far more — this is a
business of a few hundred people in your region, not a business of reach. Spend
80% of reputation effort on 1–3 and treat public posting as distribution.

### On CFA Level I

You hold a 2025 access scholarship [V, A12]. Level I is roughly 300 hours.
Against your six-month plan (186 portfolio hours + networking), it does not fit
alongside everything else.

**[REC]** Register for a sitting that falls *after* your expected authorisation
date, and use the scholarship. Do not let it compete with the portfolio in months
1–6 — the portfolio is what gets you the conversation, and Level I is what makes
you slightly more legible in a resume screen you have already decided not to
compete in (`02` §1, Decision 1). **[I]** If forced to choose: portfolio first.
Level I would help most in path #12 (asset management), which ranks 52.

**[I]** The month-6 reconnect sweep is the most important single row. Its purpose

last month — so your "I'm authorised now" message lands as continuation, not
resurrection.
