# PART VIII — Conversation Portfolio

The goal is not to display knowledge. It is to be the person who asks the
question the other party finds interesting — because that is what earns a second
conversation.

**[I] The asymmetry that governs this file:** you will never out-know a partner
about their own market. You *can* know one narrow thing better than they expect,
and you can ask questions that reveal you have actually done the work. A
23-year-old who says "I pulled the County Business Patterns data for this NAICS
in the four counties — there are fewer companies in the 20–99 band than I
expected, and I'm trying to work out whether that's consolidation or
misclassification" is instantly a different category of person from one who says
"I'm really interested in private equity."

## §2. Ten industries — but learn three properly

**[I]** Ten industries at conversational depth is roughly 120 hours and produces
shallow knowledge in all of them. Three at real depth beats ten at cocktail
depth, every time, because depth is what survives a follow-up question.

**Learn properly (25 hrs each):**
1. **Commercial & industrial services** — your Project 1 sector; the one you can
   speak about from experience [V, A10]
2. **Logistics & 3PL** — regionally dominant; TQL and Taylor are live examples [R]
3. **Specialty manufacturing** — the classic Midwest LMM buyout sector

**Know the shape of (3 hrs each):** healthcare services · insurance & insurance
services · consumer packaged goods · financial data & analytics (you already have
a capstone's worth [V, A8]) · construction services · distribution · SaaS/vertical software

**Per-industry framework:** How does a company here make money? What is the unit?
Where does operating leverage come from? What kills companies here? What does a
buyer pay and why? How fragmented is it locally, with a cited source? Who is
already consolidating it?

## §3. Ten transactions — sourced, never recalled

⚠️ **I have deliberately not listed ten named deals.** Deal facts — price,
multiple, structure, date — are the highest-risk category for confabulation, and
misciting a transaction to a dealmaker who worked on it is unrecoverable.

**Source them yourself. Four reliable methods:**

| Method | What it yields | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **EDGAR full-text search** for `8-K` + acquisition language, filtered to Ohio filers | Public acquirers of private Cincinnati businesses — with actual purchase prices | efts.sec.gov |
| **DEFM14A merger proxies** | The full narrative: background of the merger, the banker's fairness opinion and its comparable transactions. **[I] The single best free training document in existence for how deals actually get priced.** | EDGAR |
| **Firm deal pages** — Roebling, RC Capital, Northcreek, RKCA, Silverstone, REAG | Local LMM transactions, announced by the participants themselves | firm sites |
| **ACG Cincinnati Deal Maker** event materials [R] | Which local deals the community itself considers notable | acg.org/cincinnati |

**Build the list to this shape (Sheet 6):** target · acquirer · date · sector ·
size if disclosed · structure · stated rationale · **source URL** · what you
learned · one question it raises.

**[REC]** Prioritise deals done *by firms in your CRM*. Knowing that Roebling
bought X and being able to ask why they underwrote it that way is worth more
than knowing every megadeal of 2026.

### §5. The theme that makes you memorable

**[R]** Stanford GSB's 2026 Search Fund Study reports **33.9% aggregate IRR and
4.75x ROI** across 862 funds. But secondary analyses put the aggregate nearer
**2.8x**, a Yale working paper found **no individual investor in its sample
replicated the index**, and **58% of deal-level returns were under 2x** [R].
Meanwhile only **48% of the 2021–2024 cohorts had acquired a company**, against
58% since 1996 [R].

**[I]** Being able to say *"the headline index return is real but it's an
aggregate that a handful of outcomes dominate, and the recent cohorts are
acquiring at a lower rate — which makes me more interested in the LDE structure
than the classic two-year search"* does something no amount of enthusiasm does:
it demonstrates that you read past the marketing number. In a room full of people
who quote 4.75x, you are the one who read the fine print.

⚠️ Verify these figures directly at
<https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/2026-search-fund-study-selected-observations>
before quoting them. Secondary numbers came from lower-tier sources.

## §6. Three questions per topic — the actual mechanism

Rules: never ask what a website answers; ask about **judgment**, not facts; ask
about their *hardest* case, not their best.

**To an LMM PE partner**
1. "When you're underwriting an owner-operated business, which add-back do you
   argue about most — and which one do you refuse regardless of the argument?"
2. "How much of your last fund's return came from multiple expansion versus
   operating improvement? Has that changed what you're willing to pay?"
3. "What's the smallest EBITDA you'll look at now, and what changed it?"

**To an independent sponsor**
1. "How do you sequence a capital provider conversation against an LOI — do you
   go to capital before or after you've locked the target?"
2. "What's the deal you walked away from that you still think about?"
3. "Where does your proprietary flow actually come from — brokers, or something
   you built?"

**To a private credit / mezzanine investor**
1. "Which covenant actually protects you in an LMM services business, and which
   one is theatre?"
2. "When a borrower trips, how early do you usually see it in the reporting —
   and what's the tell?"
3. "How do you get comfortable with customer concentration below $10M of EBITDA?"

**To an M&A boutique MD**
1. "What makes an owner ready to sell versus just curious?"
2. "How much does the quality of the QoE change your process now versus five years ago?"
3. "Which buyer type is actually easiest to close — strategic, sponsor, or independent sponsor?"

**To a family-office principal**
1. "How do you decide between a direct deal and committing to a fund?"
2. "What do you need from an operating partner that you find hardest to hire?"
3. "How do you think about time horizon differently from a fund?"

**To a searcher / LDE operator**
1. "What's your acquisition criteria, and how has it narrowed since you started?"
2. "Where is your sourcing actually coming from — and what's your response rate?"
3. "How are you thinking about the LDE structure versus a classic search?"

**To a business owner (Goering Center rooms)**
1. "What's the part of running this that nobody warned you about?"
2. "When people approach you about buying, what makes you take the call?"
3. "What would have to be true for you to think about a transition?"

**[I]** The last question in each set is the one that produces a real
conversation, because it asks about something the person is genuinely uncertain
about. Uncertainty is what people want to talk about; competence is what they are
tired of performing.
