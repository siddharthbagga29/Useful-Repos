# PART XI & XII — Daily and Weekly Operating System

The checklist version is in `00-START-HERE.md`. This file is the SOP behind it.

---

## §1. Morning SOP — 2h 15m

### Step 1 · Open the Dashboard (5 min)
Read **only the red block** (rows 6–10). Everything else on that sheet is for
Sunday. If all five red counts are zero, you are ahead; go straight to step 4.

### Step 2 · The system tells you what needs attention
The Dashboard and today's Daily Tasks rows answer, without you deciding anything:
- who needs follow-up (overdue count, and the rows are red in Sheet 2)
- who to contact new (proposed only when the follow-up queue is under three)
- what event is upcoming, and whether its 48-hour pre-brief is done
- which research question is due
- which skill rep is due
- which project needs progress, with hours and % quoted back at you

### Step 3 · Approve or reject (5 min)
Set Approved = Yes/No on each proposed row. **Rejecting is expected and healthy** —
if you approve everything every day, the engine's thresholds are too loose and
you should say so in Sunday's review. Target: approve 3, reject the rest.

### Step 4 · Relationship block (45 min)
Follow-ups first, in tier order, always. Then one new contact.
- Before sending, read the `Value I Can Give` cell. If it is empty, **fill it
  before you write the message.** A follow-up with no value attached is the
  message that teaches someone to stop replying.
- Log immediately: Last Contact, Next Contact (= today + Cadence Days),
  Conversation Topic.

### Step 5 · Reputation / investment block (60–90 min)
One named deliverable on the live project, or the day's research question.
Every external fact acquired here gets a row in Sheet 15 **at the moment you read
it**, not later. Later does not happen.

### Step 6 · Verify (inside step 5, not after)
Nothing enters Sheets 2, 4 or 6 as VERIFIED without a primary-source URL and
today's date. See `19-verification-protocol.md`.

### Step 7 · Close out (15 min)
- Update every CRM row you touched
- Update project hours and %
- Set the next action for anyone contacted today
- Generate tomorrow's tasks; approve three
- Close the laptop

### The 30-minute collapse rule
On a day that falls apart: **do the follow-ups and nothing else.** Portfolio work
postponed is delayed. A relationship allowed to go cold is destroyed, and it
takes the whole prior investment with it. This is the one asymmetry in the system
worth memorising.

## §2. The three daily priorities

| Slot | Question | Success looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **RELATIONSHIP** | Who do I contact, and what do I give them? | 2–4 follow-ups sent, 1 new contact, all logged |
| **REPUTATION** | What did I produce today that is evidence of judgment? | One named deliverable, or one research entry with a reusable talking point |
| **OPPORTUNITY** | What did I investigate that could become a door? | A company, a sector cut, a person's portfolio, an event |

**[I]** The order is not arbitrary. Relationships have the longest lead time, so
they must start earliest and never lapse. Reputation compounds but tolerates a
missed day. Opportunity is downstream of both.

## §3. Friday sweep — 30 min

```
☐ Network      Who went cold? (sort Sheet 2 by Days Since Contact, descending)
               Who moved a tier? Update Relationship Strength honestly.
☐ Opportunities Advance or kill every row in Sheet 12. Killing is progress.
☐ Investment   Is the live project on pace? If not, CUT SCOPE — never extend the date.
☐ Skills       Which reps are overdue? Do one now or reschedule it deliberately.
☐ Financial    Statement dates this week? Utilization at statement? Runway months?
```

**[I]** "Cut scope, never extend the date" is the rule that keeps six projects
finishable. A project that slips twice will slip forever; a project that shrinks
still ships, and a shipped 8-page memo beats an unshipped 20-page one in every
conversation you will ever have.

## §4. Sunday review — 45 min

1. Menu → **Compute this week's score**. Read the number.
2. Fill the columns the script cannot know: follow-ups sent, posts, technical
   hours, communication hours, savings, utilization, emergency-fund months.
3. Answer the improvement questions (§5).
4. **Remove one recurring task.** Every week. Non-negotiable.
5. Set next week's five targets.
6. Schedule events; put each pre-brief on the calendar 48 hours before.
7. Select or continue the project; write its next deliverable down.

### Reading the score
| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 75–100 | Excellent and probably not repeatable. Note what made it possible. |
| 55–74 | The target band. This is what sustainable looks like. |
| 35–54 | Acceptable during a heavy portfolio week. Two in a row is a signal. |
| < 35 | Something is wrong. Diagnose with §5, do not just resolve to try harder. |

**[I]** The failure mode here is treating the score as a grade. It is a
diagnostic. A 45 in a week you finished a credit memo is a good week; a 70 built
from twenty shallow LinkedIn contacts is a bad one. That is exactly why the
components are capped (`10-sheets-architecture.md` §Weekly score).

## §5. The self-improvement loop

Eight questions, every Sunday, in Weekly Review columns S:Y.

1. **What produced conversations?** (which channel, which script, which room)
2. **What produced introductions?** (the highest-value output there is)
3. **What produced opportunities?**
4. **What research turned out to be useful** — i.e. did you actually say it to a
   human? Research nobody heard is a hobby.
5. **What was wasted effort?**
6. **What automation failed or annoyed me?**
7. **What should be removed?**
8. **What should be doubled down on?**

### The removal rule
One recurring task leaves the system every week. **[I]** Systems die of
accretion, not neglect: each week's small addition is individually defensible and
collectively fatal. Forcing a removal means the system has to keep justifying
every part of itself. If you genuinely cannot find one to cut, cut the least
valuable one anyway and see whether you miss it.

### The month-2 audit
At week 8, look back at weeks 1–8 and ask the only question that matters: **which
single activity produced the most second conversations?** Then reallocate hours
toward it, even if that means abandoning something in this repo. This system is a
hypothesis about what works for you; eight weeks of your own data beats it.

## §6. Personal knowledge management — deliberately boring

**Google Drive + Google Sheets. Nothing else.** Not Notion, not Obsidian, not
Roam.

**[I]** The reason is not that those tools are bad — it is that a second system
requires syncing, and every personal knowledge system that has ever died, died of
sync debt. Your CRM must be where your automation lives, your automation is in
Sheets, therefore your knowledge lives in Sheets and Drive.

```
/Positioning OS
  /01 Projects
      /P1 Sector Thesis      ← memo, workbook, /sources with every downloaded PDF
      /P2 LBO
      /P3 Private Credit Memo
      /P4 QoE Case
      /P5 Public Valuation
      /P6 Acquisition Criteria
  /02 Research
      /Companies             ← one doc per company, named "NAME - YYYY-MM-DD"
      /Industries
      /Transactions
  /03 People                 ← one doc per A-tier contact only. Not per contact.
  /04 Materials              ← résumé versions, bio, story scripts, portfolio index
  /05 Financial
  /06 Immigration            ← counsel correspondence, receipts, filing copies
  /07 Archive
```

Four rules that make this work:
1. **Date every filename.** `Cintas notes - 2026-09-15`. Version confusion is the
   main way documents rot.
2. **Meeting notes go in the CRM row, not a document** — unless the person is
   A-tier. One place to look.
3. **Every downloaded source PDF goes in the project's `/sources` folder** at the
   moment you download it, with the URL in Sheet 15.
4. **Archive, never delete.** `/07 Archive` costs nothing.

## §7. Minimum viable day

When everything is on fire, this is the system:

```
☐ 10 min  Open Dashboard. Send the overdue follow-ups. Nothing else.
```

**[I]** That is the whole minimum. A system with a genuine 10-minute floor
survives a bad month; one with a 2-hour floor gets abandoned in week seven and
you lose the compounding. **[R]** Lally et al. (2010) found automaticity took a
median of 66 days among the 48% of participants who reached it, with a range of
18–254 — and, importantly, that **missing a single day did not derail habit
formation**. The point is not the 66 days, which is widely over-quoted; it is
that the recovery property is real, so design for recovery rather than for
streaks.
