## 1. What you're actually buying

**Fidelity watchlist — ~31 unique tickers:**
FREL, SCHD, JEPQ, FSKAX, JEPI, O, VT, ABBV, AAPL, QYLD, NVDA, VNQ, GLD, MSFT, BLK, XYLD, SCHG,

**Robinhood — 5 tickers:** AMD(?), ASML, NVDA, TSM, AVGO

At $150 biweekly across 31 names that is **$4.84 per position per contribution**. That is not a
portfolio — it is a collection. And it's worse than it looks, because the 31 names are not 31 bets.

### Problem A: the same exposure, bought four times

- **VT** = the entire global stock market. It already *contains* VXUS, and it already contains
  everything in FSKAX.
- So **VT + VXUS + FSKAX + FSPSX** is buying the world, then buying half the world again, then
  buying the US again.
- **QQQ + SCHG + AAPL + MSFT + NVDA** — QQQ and SCHG are both large-cap growth, and both are already
  ~20–40% AAPL/MSFT/NVDA. Buying the singles on top triples down on the same three companies.
- **SCHD + DGRO + VIG + SDY + DIVO** — five US dividend funds with heavy mutual overlap.
- **FREL + VNQ + O + STAG** — two REIT index funds plus two individual REITs those funds already hold.

You have roughly **six real bets** (US market, international, growth/tech, dividend value, REITs,
gold) spread across 31 tickers. The extra tickers add admin and tax-lot complexity, not diversification.

### Problem B: five covered-call funds, and this one is expensive

**JEPI, JEPQ, QYLD, XYLD, DIVO.** These systematically sell away your upside in exchange for
current income. For someone drawing retirement cash that's a legitimate trade. For someone
**accumulating over 5–30 years, it is the wrong instrument**, and the magnitude isn't subtle:

> **QYLD over 10 years: compounded at 9.83%/yr. QQQ over the same 10 years: 20.59%/yr.**
> QYLD's share price is down ~35% from a decade ago while the Nasdaq tripled. Since QYLD's
> Dec-2013 inception: QQQ +737% on price alone, QYLD +178% *with distributions reinvested*.

A $10,000 QYLD investment at launch paid out ~$14,000 in cumulative distributions — but the shares
are now worth ~$6,500. The income was real. It was substantially your own capital handed back.

**And the tax:** JEPI/JEPQ distributions are largely taxed as **ordinary income** (up to 37%), not
qualified dividends (0–20%). In a taxable account you're paying the highest rate on income generated
by a strategy that's already capping your growth. QYLD/XYLD have similar issues.

### Problem C: this is the AI concentration again

AMD + ASML + NVDA + TSM + AVGO is a **single-sector bet on semiconductors** — and every one of them
depends on the same AI-capex cycle. Layer QQQ, SCHG, NVDA, MSFT and AAPL from the Fidelity side and
your true tech/AI exposure is far above what a 31-name list suggests.

You told me last time that the AI names went against you. **This allocation increases that exposure,
it doesn't hedge it.** Owning it via five tickers instead of one doesn't diversify it — semis rise
and fall together, especially in a drawdown, which is exactly when you need them not to.

|---|---|
| 6% | **43.0 years** |
| 8% | **35.9 years** |
| 10% | **31.1 years** |

**$200 biweekly does not get you to $1M on any horizon you'd call "early."** It gets you there
around retirement age, and at 6% it may not get you there in a working lifetime.

This is not a criticism of $200 — it's a real contribution and far better than nothing. It's just
that $5,200/year is roughly 0.5% of $1,000,000. Compounding has to do close to *all* the work, and
that takes decades. No stock selection fixes this. **The gap is arithmetic, not strategy.**

## 5. Free money you're leaving on the table

**Your entire $5,200/year fits inside a single IRA.** The 2026 IRA contribution limit is **$7,500**
($8,600 with the $1,100 catch-up if 50+). The 2026 401(k) limit is **$24,500**.

This matters more than any ticker decision in this document:

- Every covered-call distribution (JEPI, JEPQ, QYLD, XYLD, DIVO) that's taxed as ordinary income in
  a brokerage account is **taxed at 0% inside an IRA**. You currently hold the most
  tax-inefficient fund category in the most tax-inefficient account type.
- Every REIT distribution (FREL, VNQ, O, STAG) is non-qualified income — same problem, same fix.
- **If your employer offers any 401(k) match and you're not capturing it, that is an immediate
  50–100% return** on those dollars, which beats every investment decision discussed here combined.

**Order of operations, highest value first:**
1. Capture the full employer 401(k) match — if one exists
2. Fund the IRA ($7,500 limit — your $5,200 fits entirely inside it)
3. Then taxable brokerage

You do not need to change what you buy to get this. You need to change *where* you buy it.

## 9. Bottom line

1. **Your 5-year number is ~$31,700 and your 10-year number is ~$78,000** at $200 biweekly, 8%.
   Most of that is your own deposits. That's normal and not a failure.
2. **$1M at $200 biweekly takes ~36 years at 8%.** There is no version of this where $200 biweekly
   gets you to $1M early. That's arithmetic, not pessimism.
3. **To hit $1M in 20–25 years you need roughly $500–$810 biweekly.** Start at $200 and escalate
   with every raise.
4. **Fix the account before the stocks.** Employer match, then IRA ($7,500 limit — your $5,200 fits
   inside it). The covered-call funds you hold are the worst possible thing to own in a taxable account.
5. **Cut from 31 tickers to 5–6.** The overlap is buying you complexity, not diversification.
6. **Drop the five covered-call funds.** They are built to convert growth into taxable income, which
   is the exact opposite of what a long-horizon accumulator needs.
7. **Cap the semis sleeve at 10–20%.** You've already learned this lesson once.

**The single highest-value action on this page is not a stock pick. It is opening the right account
and raising the contribution.** Everything else is worth a few percent. Those two are worth years.
