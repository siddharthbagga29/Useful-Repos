## 1. The 30% target — answered directly

You asked me to build a portfolio averaging **30%/year**. I have to give you a straight answer
before designing anything, because designing *toward* that number would hurt you.

**30% sustained annual returns over a long horizon is not achievable, by anyone, reliably.**

Run your own plan at 30% and see why:

| Horizon | @30%/yr | @10%/yr |
|---|---|---|
| 5 yr | $56,746 | $34,656 |
| 10 yr | $264,192 | $89,062 |
| 20 yr | $3,894,250 | $317,796 |
| 30 yr | **$53,937,677** | $911,074 |

$200 biweekly at 30% for 30 years makes you one of the wealthiest individuals alive — from
$5,200/year. That's the tell. **Warren Buffett compounded at roughly 19.8%/year over ~60 years and
that is the best sustained record in the history of public markets.** The S&P 500's long-run average
is ~10%.

30% *happens* — in single years, in single stocks, in bull markets. It is not a rate you can plan
around. The danger isn't that you'll miss the target; it's that reaching for 30% forces leverage,
options, concentration and momentum-chasing — the exact behaviours that produce permanent capital
loss. You already told me the AI names went against you. That was a mild version of this.

**What I'll do instead:** build the highest realistic long-run expected return available to a
long-horizon accumulator, using evidence-backed tilts rather than hope. That number is **~9%, with
a 7–11% band.** Everything below is optimised for that, and I'll show exactly where each basis
point comes from.
