## 1. The single most important thing in this review

You told me the AI names went against you, and you want to keep going without panic-selling.
That instinct is correct. But understand what this basket actually is:

**EXE, AR, and EQT are not a diversifier away from the AI trade. They ARE the AI trade,
expressed through a different instrument.**

Dry-gas E&Ps have been re-rated over the past two years substantially on the thesis that AI data
centers need firm, non-intermittent power, that power is gas-fired, and gas demand therefore
structurally rises. Baker Hughes is explicit about it — it has flagged a ~$3B data-center-related
order opportunity across 2025–2027 and is doubling NovaLT turbine capacity by 1H27 with output
effectively committed through 2028.

So if you buy this basket equal-weight, half of it (EXE + AR + EQT = 50%) sits on one macro factor —
Henry Hub, levered to the same AI-capex narrative that already hurt you. **Same bet, different ticker.**
That is concentration dressed up as diversification, and it's the trap worth avoiding here.

The parts of this basket that genuinely diversify you are ET (fee-based, contracted, volume-driven),
BKR (~60% of revenue now infrastructure/industrial, not upstream services), and DVN (oil-weighted).

## 2. The macro: the thesis is real, the timing is not free

This is the crux, and it cuts both ways.

**Bullish, structurally:** record LNG export capacity ramping (Plaquemines, Corpus Christi Stage 3,
Golden Pass), LNG exports forecast +11% (~1.7 Bcf/d) in 2027, AI/data-center power demand,
declining Haynesville rig counts.

**Bearish, right now:** spot prices have been languishing near or below $3/MMBtu. The 12-month strip
settled at $3.19/MMBtu on 2 September, *down* from $3.22 a month earlier. And the critical data point:

> **The EIA cut its 2027 Henry Hub forecast from $4.60/MMBtu to roughly $3.18–3.28.**
> That is a ~30% downgrade to the out-year price deck.

Why: production growth running at ~124 Bcf/d, storage above seasonal norms, and a Permian associated-gas
flood. Permian gas prices have been *deeply negative* on constrained takeaway, and **more than 5 Bcf/d
of new Permian egress capacity comes online within 12 months** — which unlocks a wave of supply that
offsets much of the LNG-driven tightening.

**Translation:** the demand story is intact and probably underestimated for 2029–2032. The supply
response is arriving *faster* than the demand. That is precisely the shape of setup that produces a
good 5-year outcome and a painful 18-month one.

You are already seeing it in the numbers, not just the forecast. Expand Energy's Q2 2026: revenue
**up** 5.4% YoY to $2.96B, but EPS **down** to $2.19 from $4.07, and net income **down 46%** to $522M.
Revenue up, earnings nearly halved. That is margin compression from a weaker realized price deck,
happening now — not a projection.

## 3. Company-by-company

### EQT Corporation (EQT) — ~$53 · widest discount in the basket
The strongest risk-adjusted name of the three gas E&Ps.

- 2026 FCF target **$3.5B**; delivered a record **$1.95B FCF in Q1 2026**, beating consensus by 17%.
- Mid-year: production guidance **raised**, capex guidance **lowered**. That combination is the
  single most reliable tell of genuine operational quality in E&P.
- Vertical integration via the Equitrans acquisition means EQT owns its own midstream — it captures
  the gathering/transport margin instead of paying it away. This is a structural cost advantage its
  peers do not have, and it's why EQT's cost floor is lower in a weak-price tape.
- 24 of 25 analysts rate it Buy. Trades ~24% below the $70 Morningstar fair value.
- **The LNG option:** management estimates that if its LNG portfolio were fully operational today,
  2026 FCF would be ~$6B rather than $3.5B. Those contracts start around **2030** and could swing
  from a $500M to a $2.5B annual FCF uplift. That is real, large, and *four years away* — which means
  it is a reason to hold for a decade, not a reason to expect a move next quarter.
- **Risk:** pure Appalachian dry gas. Almost no commodity diversification. A sub-$3 Henry Hub for
  two years hurts directly.

**Verdict: the highest-conviction name here.** Best asset quality, best cost structure, widest discount.

### Expand Energy (EXE) — ~$94 · scale, but earnings already compressing
- Largest independent US natural gas producer by net daily production. ~7.48 Bcfe/d in Q2 2026,
  ~92% natural gas. Marcellus + Haynesville.
- Trades around 8x trailing earnings. Consensus PT ~$125–130 (25–26 analysts, Buy); Mizuho at $136.
- Dividend $0.57/quarter, ~2.4% yield.
- Priced $500M of 5.65% senior notes due 2031, closing ~17 Sep 2026 — routine balance-sheet management,
  not distress, but note it's terming out debt at a mid-5s coupon.
- **Risk:** the Q2 earnings drop (−46% net income YoY) is the clearest evidence in this basket that
  the weak gas deck is already biting. And 92% gas means essentially zero hedge within the business.
  Scale is a cost advantage; it is not a commodity-price hedge.

**Verdict: good asset, real business, but the most exposed to being early.** Buy it slowly.

### Antero Resources (AR) — ~$41 · highest beta, NGLs are the differentiator
- ~11x trailing / ~9.2x forward P/E, EV/EBITDA ~7.1. Below the oil & gas industry average (~14.3x).
- TTM revenue $5.78B, net income $1.08B. 52-week range $29.10–$45.75 — a **57% spread**, the most
  volatile name in the basket by a wide margin. Market cap ~$12.8B. +20.5% over 52 weeks.
- FY2026 production guidance raised to 4.1 Bcfe/d, ~20% above 2025. 14 analysts Buy, 0 Sell.
- **Why it's genuinely different:** heavy NGL exposure. NGL pricing tracks petrochemical and export
  demand, not Henry Hub, so AR is the one gas name with a partially independent revenue driver.
- **Risks:** EV/FCF ~21.5x is notably rich against its EV/EBITDA of 7.1 — meaning the earnings look
  cheap but the *cash conversion* does not. That gap is the thing to watch. Plus the fair value
  estimate conflict flagged above, and the highest volatility here.

**Verdict: smallest position of the three gas names.** Best upside if gas turns, worst drawdown if it doesn't.

### Devon Energy (DVN) — ~$51 · the genuine diversifier
- **Oil-weighted, Permian-focused** — the only real commodity diversification in the basket.
- FY2026: ~1.38 MMBoe/d production, ~$4.9B capex, >60% into the Permian.
- Returns up to **70% of FCF** to shareholders: $0.32 quarterly dividend (~2.49% yield, $1.28/yr)
  plus an **$8B buyback**.
- UBS raised its PT to $63 (from $55); Raymond James to $67 (from $64).
- Trades ~8–15% below the $56 Morningstar fair value — the *narrowest* discount here, so it's the
  least "cheap," but it's also the least correlated to the gas thesis.
- The Coterra transaction adds roughly $4/share to fair value.
- **Risk:** oil has its own cycle — OPEC+ policy, global demand. Different risk, not less risk.

**Verdict: hold it precisely because it isn't a gas stock.** Its job here is correlation reduction.

### Baker Hughes (BKR) — ~$59 · the highest-quality business in the basket
- **Record IET backlog: $37B** at end-Q2 2026. Record Q2 bookings of **$7.1B**, driven by Power
  Systems and LNG.
- FY2026 IET orders guidance **raised to $17.5–19.5B**; the 2026–2028 outlook lifted to **over $45B**.
- ~**60% of revenue now comes from infrastructure and industrial markets**, not traditional upstream
  services. This is the key fact: BKR has structurally de-risked itself away from the drilling cycle.
- Closed the **$13.6B Chart Industries acquisition on 16 July 2026** — cryogenic equipment, serving
  gas infrastructure, nuclear, data centers, carbon capture, space, geothermal. Targeting $325M of
  annualized cost synergies by year three.
- Trades at $59 against a 52-week high of $70.41 — **~16% off the high**, and ~19% below the $73
  Morningstar fair value.
- **Risk:** management itself acknowledged near-term pressure from Chart integration at the Barclays
  conference in September. A $13.6B deal is a real execution risk, and the market is pricing some of it.

**Verdict: the best business here, and the pullback is the opportunity.** Sells picks and shovels to
the whole thesis without taking direct commodity price risk. It gets paid whether gas is $3 or $5.

### Energy Transfer (ET) — ~$21 · the ballast
- **2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance raised to $18.8–19.1B**, up ~$0.5B at the midpoint.
- **Distribution coverage 2.28x** in H1 2026 — $5.29B DCF against $2.32B of distributions. That is
  an exceptionally strong coverage ratio for a midstream MLP and it is the number that matters most.
- ~$1.36/unit TTM, quarterly $0.34, yield ~6.3–6.7%. Targeting 3–5% annual distribution growth while
  coverage stays near 2x. 2027 payout projected ~$1.416/unit.
- Fee-based, volume-driven — **largely insulated from the commodity price** that threatens the E&Ps.
- Trades ~11% below the $24 Morningstar fair value; a top pick in Morningstar's midstream coverage.
- ⚠️ **TAX WARNING — read this before you buy:** ET is a **limited partnership**, not a corporation.
  It issues a **Schedule K-1**, not a 1099. That means (a) your tax filing gets meaningfully more
  complex and K-1s often arrive late, sometimes after the filing deadline; (b) holding it in an
  **IRA/retirement account can generate UBTI**, which can create a tax bill *inside* a
  tax-advantaged account. If you're in Europe, also check local treatment of US MLPs and withholding —
  it is frequently unfavourable and may make ET the wrong instrument for you specifically, however
  good the business is.

**Verdict: the best business-quality-to-price ratio for income, with a genuine administrative catch.**
The 2.28x coverage means the distribution is safe. Resolve the K-1/tax question first.

## 4. Should you invest? — direct answer

**Yes to the basket, no to equal weighting, and not on the 43% premise.**

The structural thesis — LNG exports plus AI-driven power demand meeting a decade of underinvestment —
is real, and I think it's underestimated on a 5–10 year view. But the 2027 supply picture has
deteriorated materially (EIA cutting its deck ~30%, 5 Bcf/d of Permian egress arriving), so the
near-to-medium term is genuinely unfavourable. This is a "right thesis, early timing" setup.

**That is exactly the situation dollar-cost averaging is designed for**, which is why your instinct
to accumulate rather than lump-sum is the correct one. Your AI positions went down because you bought
a correct long-term thesis at a full valuation. Don't repeat the mistake by buying a correct long-term
thesis at 50% concentration in one factor.

## 6. Biweekly or monthly, and how much

### Frequency: **biweekly**, but the edge is small

With fractional shares and zero commissions, biweekly costs nothing extra and gives you ~26 entry
points a year instead of 12 — more averaging across volatility, which matters more than usual in a
basket this volatile (AR's 52-week range is 57% wide). If biweekly matches your pay cycle, it also
removes the temptation to time entries, which is the actual benefit.

**Honest caveat: the mathematical advantage of biweekly over monthly is small.** If monthly is easier
to sustain, choose monthly. *Consistency beats frequency.* The worst outcome is an aggressive biweekly
schedule you abandon in month four.

### Amount: the framework, then the numbers

Your $10 order is a mechanics test, not a position. $1.67 per name won't compound into anything — but
it will confirm the fractional-share execution works, which is a legitimate first step.

Three rules for sizing the real thing:

1. **Only money you will not need for 5+ years.** This thesis pays off on LNG and data-center buildout
   timelines. EQT's LNG uplift starts around 2030. If you might need this money in 2028, this is the
   wrong basket entirely.
2. **This whole basket should be ≤10–15% of your total portfolio.** It is one sector with a heavily
   correlated internal structure. You already have AI exposure that correlates with it — count that
   toward the same risk budget.
3. **Size so a 40% drawdown doesn't change your behaviour.** Assume it happens; gas E&Ps have done it
   repeatedly. If the number you're contemplating would make a 40% paper loss unbearable, halve it.
   The right amount is the amount you can keep contributing *through* the drawdown — that's where DCA
   actually earns its return.

I've deliberately not told you which column is yours — that depends on income, emergency fund, and
existing portfolio size, none of which I know. Pick the column where a 40% drawdown is genuinely
survivable, then **automate it and stop watching the daily prices.**

### Reinvest the income
ET at ~6.4% and DVN/EXE at ~2.4–2.5% throw off real cash. Turn on dividend reinvestment. In a sector
this cyclical, reinvested distributions during the *down* years are historically where a large share
of the total return comes from. This is the part most retail investors skip.

## 7. What would make me wrong

Track these, not the daily price:

- **Henry Hub sustained below $2.75** through winter 2026–27 → the E&P thesis breaks; stop adding to
  EXE/AR/EQT and let ET/BKR carry the basket.
- **Permian egress completing on schedule** (5+ Bcf/d) with production filling it → bearish; the
  supply wave lands.
- **LNG facility startups slipping** (Plaquemines, Corpus Christi Stage 3, Golden Pass) → removes the
  demand leg. Watch these dates specifically.
- **BKR IET backlog stalling below ~$37B**, or Chart synergies missing the $325M path → sell BKR;
  the backlog *is* the thesis.
- **ET coverage falling below ~1.8x** → the distribution safety margin is eroding. At 2.28x there's
  enormous room, so this would be a genuine surprise and a genuine signal.
- **EQT reverting to raising capex while cutting production guidance** → the operational quality
  argument dies.

**Upside triggers:** a cold winter drawing down storage; an LNG facility ramping ahead of schedule;
a large data-center power purchase agreement signed directly with a gas producer.

## 8. Bottom line

The basket is worth owning. The thesis is sound on a 5–10 year horizon and these are, with the
exception of the fair-value premise, reasonably-valued businesses. EQT and BKR are the two I'd hold
with most conviction — EQT for the discount and the cost structure, BKR for the business quality and
the $37B backlog.

But **fix the premise before you fix the portfolio.** You're buying on a "43% off" number that isn't
real, into a sector where the out-year price deck was just cut 30%, with 50% of the basket on the same
macro factor that already hurt you in AI. Correct the weighting, accept that you may well be early,
size it so being early doesn't force you to sell, and let the biweekly contributions do the work.

Being early is only a mistake if you're forced to sell before you're proven right. **Size for that.**
