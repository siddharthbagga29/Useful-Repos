## 3. The 19.8% question — what Buffett actually did

This is worth answering properly, because the real answer is more useful than a flat "no."

Frazzini, Kabiller & Pedersen decomposed Berkshire's record in *Buffett's Alpha* (Financial Analysts
Journal, 2018). Findings:

1. **Sharpe ratio of 0.79.** Excellent — roughly double the market's — but not otherworldly.
2. **His alpha becomes statistically insignificant** once you control for two factors:
   **Betting-Against-Beta** (low-volatility stocks) and **Quality-Minus-Junk** (profitable, stable,
   growing companies).
3. **Leverage of ~1.6–1.7x on average**, financed by **insurance float** — policyholder premiums
   held before claims are paid. Cheap, permanent, and it cannot be margin-called.
4. Conclusion, in the authors' words: his returns are *"reward for leveraging cheap, safe, quality
   stocks."*

**So 19.8% = a 0.79-Sharpe portfolio × 1.6x free leverage × 60 years.**

### What's replicable and what isn't

| Component | Available to you? |
|---|---|
| Quality factor (QMJ) | ✅ Yes — AVUV screens on **profitability** as well as value. This is literally the quality tilt. |
| Value factor | ✅ Yes — AVUV + SCHD |
| Low-beta / BAB | ⚠️ Partially — via low-volatility funds, but it works *because* of leverage |
| **1.6x leverage at insurance-float cost** | ❌ **No.** This is the whole ballgame and it is not purchasable. |
| 60-year uninterrupted horizon | ⚠️ Only if you start now and never sell |
| Permanent capital nobody can redeem | ❌ No |

**The leverage is the part you cannot buy.** Margin costs far more than float, and gets called at
exactly the wrong moment — a margin call in a drawdown converts a paper loss into a permanent one.
Applying 1.6x to a 6% portfolio does not produce 19.8%; it produces a 6% portfolio that can go to
zero.

**You already own the replicable part.** AVUV *is* the quality-value tilt. Raising it from 3.4% to
20% is the single most Buffett-like move available to you — and you can only do it once.
