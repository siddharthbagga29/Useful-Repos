# 02 — Idea scorecard

Score 1–10. Weights: pain 20%, budget 15%, speed to first dollar 15%, my edge 20%, low legal burden 10%, scalability 10%, low build cost 10%.

| Idea | Pain | Budget | Speed | Edge | Legal | Scale | Cost | **Score** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A. **Small-Deal Underwriting Toolkit** — Excel model + case library + checklists + short course for first-time buyers of $1–10M businesses | 6 | 5 | 8 | 9 | 8 | 6 | 9 | **7.0** |
| B. Fire inspection app | 6 | 6 | 3 | 5 | 8 | 6 | 3 | **5.1** |
| C. "QoE-lite" underwriting service for sub-$5M deals (productized, $1.5–5k) | 7 | 6 | 6 | 8 | 4 | 5 | 7 | **6.3** |

Math: A = .2·6+.15·5+.15·8+.2·9+.1·8+.1·6+.1·9 = 7.0. These are my judgments, not data.

## Decision
- **Test A first.** It reuses assets you already built (model, 20 cases, workbook, checklists), costs almost nothing, has the lightest legal burden.
- **C is the follow-on** if A shows demand: higher price, but services touching valuation/QoE need CPA/licensing review and take your time.
- **B killed.**

## Honest limits
- A digital toolkit is easy to copy and low-priced (assume $49–$299). $1,000,000 revenue would need thousands of buyers or a move up to C/consulting/SaaS. Treat A as the cheapest way to learn what people pay for.
- ASSUMPTION: aspiring acquirers pay for tools. Test before building more.

## Kill rules (decide before you start)
1. Fewer than 5 of 15 target interviewees say they'd use it AND have paid for something similar → kill A.
2. Fewer than 3 pre-orders at the test price within 3 weeks of a live offer → change or kill.
3. More than $500 spent before the first pre-order → stop.
