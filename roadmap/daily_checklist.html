Every day: Start. Focus. Connect. Close.

1 · Start 5 min · before anything else

  • Phone in another room (or airplane mode) until Block 3
  • Read today's 3 goals. Write the one that makes today a win: ______________
  • Write the plan: "At ____ , at my desk, I will open ______." (when, where, what)
  • Clear the desk. One notebook, one screen, one tab
  • Water and 2 minutes of movement

2 · Focus blocks total 60–120 min

  • Block 1 · Career (20–45 min) — start: ______ end: ______
  • Block 2 · Acquisition + learning loop (30–60 min) — start: ______ end: ______
  • Block 3 · Network / money (15–30 min) — start: ______ end: ______
  • Timer on for every block. Stop when it rings, even if it's going well
  • Break 5 min: stand, water, no phone, no feeds

3 · Learning loop inside Block 2 · see p.2

  • Preview 3 min: headings and questions only
  • Learn 20 min: one topic, notes closed after
  • Recall 5 min: write what I remember with the source closed
  • Check 3 min: fix what I got wrong
  • Use 5 min: build, calculate or explain it aloud

4 · Connect 15 min · Block 3

  • 1 follow-up to someone I already spoke with (follow-ups first)
  • 1 new person found from a primary source (team page, event list). URL saved
  • 1 give: share a useful article, memo or thank-you. Ask for nothing
  • Log every touch in the CRM: date, topic, next step
  • Never ask for a job. Ask about their judgment

When I want to switch the urge is the signal, not the instruction

  1. Name it: "I'm uncomfortable; I want relief."
  2. Write the exact thing that feels hard on paper (1 line).
  3. Shrink it: do only the next 2 minutes of it.
  4. Put the new idea on the Parking Lot. Don't open it.
  5. Breathe slowly 5 times, then restart the timer.
  6. Still stuck after 10 min? Ask a question out loud or on paper, then move to the next step of the same task, not a new task.

5 · Close 5 min · before bed or after Block 3

  • Tick the goals. Score today: 0 1 2 3
  • Write 3 things I learned without looking at notes
  • Put today's topics on the review schedule (p.2)
  • Parking Lot: ____________________ (not started)
  • Set tomorrow's "At ___ I will open ___" plan
  • Phone charges outside the bedroom. Lights out by ______
Rule of the week: Never miss twice. A missed day doesn't undo a habit; skipping the restart does. On a bad day do only Block 3's follow-up and the evening close.

How: the 5-step routine

  2. Message (template below). One question, 2-minute answer.
  3. Follow up after 5–7 days, once. Then stop.
  4. After a call: thank-you within 24 hours, with one thing I learned.
  5. Ask: "Who else should I talk to?" and log the referral.

B · The habit of learning anything fast

No shortcut exists, but three things speed it up the most (well-supported in the research on how people learn; confirm in the sources at right):

  1. Test yourself instead of re-reading.
  2. Space it out: review the same idea on later days.
  3. Use it: build something, explain it, or teach it.

C · Why I jump to something easier

When a task is hard, my brain wants relief, and switching feels like progress. The fix is not willpower; it's design:

  • Make starting tiny: "open the file and write one line."
  • Remove escape routes: phone away, one tab, site blockers on, notifications off.
  • Pre-decide: "At 7:30 at my desk I will open the case study." Writing the when, where and what raises follow-through (see evidence file 17).
  • Make the hard part visible: write the exact stuck point; most "I can't" is "I don't know the next step."
  • Work in short timed blocks (25–45 min) with real breaks.
  • Reward after, not before: a walk, a coffee, a tick on the page.
  • Protect sleep. Tired brains avoid discomfort faster.

If distraction is strong and constant despite this, mention it to a doctor. That isn't a flaw, and it's treatable.
